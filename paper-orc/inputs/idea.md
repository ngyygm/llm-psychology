# Acquiescence Is Not Personality: Why Human Psychometric Instruments Fail on LLMs

## Problem Statement

A growing literature administers human personality questionnaires (Big Five, Dark Triad, etc.) to large language models and reports the resulting scores as if they reflected genuine traits. None of this work asks the prior measurement question: do these instruments actually measure anything meaningful when applied to LLMs? In human samples, an instrument is not trusted until it passes a battery of psychometric checks (internal consistency, factor structure, convergent validity, measurement invariance). For LLMs, these checks are almost never run. If the foundations are broken, every downstream claim about "LLM personality" — including LLMs-as-silicon-samples for survey simulation — is unsupported.

## Core Hypothesis

When standard psychometric validation checks are applied to LLM responses on validated personality instruments, every check will fail, and every failure will trace back to a single mechanism: **acquiescence** — a blanket tendency to agree with statements regardless of direction, induced by safety/alignment training. Acquiescence has a directional asymmetry — positive forward-reverse gap on normal-personality items (IPIP) and negative gap on dark-trait items (SD3) — and its damage propagates from the item level to the domain level to the model level. Persona prompting will produce large, recoverable role shifts but will not restore measurement validity, because role compliance is not the same thing as trait variance.

## Method

We administered a 221-item battery combining four validated human instruments — IPIP-NEO-120 (120 Likert-5, 41 reverse), SD3 (27 Likert-5, 5 reverse), ZKPQ-50-CC (50 True/False, 12 reverse), EPQR-A (24 Yes/No, 5 reverse) — covering 17 personality domains across three response formats with 63 reverse-keyed items in total. The battery went to 20 LLMs spanning 8 families (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, DeepSeek, Alibaba, Zhipu, Moonshot, MiniMax) under 17 conditions (a Default no-persona prompt plus 16 MBTI persona prompts) at temperature 0.7, with k=5 independent samples per (model, persona, item) cell. The completed dataset contains 375,700 raw API calls (61 refusals, all under Default) yielding 75,140 averaged item-level scores. We then ran six standard psychometric validation checks: internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha per IPIP domain), factor structure (EFA on 17 domain scores with Kaiser criterion + parallel analysis + leave-one-model-out replication), reverse-item consistency (Pairwise Inconsistency Rate and per-instrument forward-vs-reverse agree-rate decomposition), variance decomposition (Model / Domain / Persona / Item / Residual SS for Likert and binary scales separately), convergent validity (8 pre-specified construct pairs), and measurement invariance (Default-vs-MBTI item-vector correlations across 320 pairs). We also stress-tested the instruments with persona steering — Persona Separation Degree, nearest-centroid persona classification with metric/scale ablations, target adherence against empirical and theoretical MBTI centroids, sign-aligned cross-scale coherence, forward/reverse directional coherence, item-level plasticity, and a factorial main-effects model for the four MBTI axes — and benchmarked the observed alphas against three synthetic baselines (Random, Pure-Acquiescence, Trait+Acquiescence). Within-sample consistency across the k=5 repeats was reported per model, persona, and domain.

## Key Contributions

- **First multi-model, multi-instrument psychometric VALIDATION study on LLMs**: 20 models × 8 families × 4 instruments × 17 personas × k=5 sampling, 375,700 API calls — orders of magnitude larger than prior LLM-personality work and explicitly diagnostic rather than profiling.
- **A single-cause causal chain from item to model.** Item-level acquiescence → Cronbach's alpha tracks reverse-item density at Spearman ρ = −1.0 across IPIP domains → exploratory factor analysis collapses 5 expected factors into 3 (universal across all 20 models, Tucker's φ = 0.993, leave-one-model-out replication is exact) → inter-model variance below 1 % on both Likert and binary scales (cross-model personality comparisons are statistically meaningless at this resolution).
- **Directional asymmetry of acquiescence.** On normal-personality items (IPIP) models exhibit a positive forward-reverse gap (Δ = +0.29); on dark-trait items (SD3) the same models reject forward items (Δ = −0.33). Acquiescence and social-desirability bias are two faces of the same alignment-driven impulse, measurable item-by-item, not separate phenomena.
- **A persona stress test that documents role compliance without trait restoration.** MBTI prompts move profiles by >1 SD per dimension (mean PSD = 1.198) and the resulting profile is 99.7 % nearest-centroid recoverable (319/320), but cross-scale coherence is only 0.839, forward/reverse pairs flip in only 69.6 % of cases, and the 3-factor collapse persists inside every persona condition. Persona steering replaces alignment-driven acquiescence with persona-driven compliance.
- **Synthetic-baseline calibration.** LLM observed alphas (0.069 – 0.928) sit closest to Random and Pure-Acquiescence baselines (α ≈ 0) and far from a Trait+Acquiescence simulation (α > 0.99). LLMs do not look like a personality with acquiescence on top — they look like acquiescence with no personality underneath.
- **An item-level plasticity map.** Persona instructions can re-color visible-behavior items (activity, sociability, rule-following, shifts up to 0.63) but cannot move safety-, morality-, or self-referential items ("Do you enjoy hurting people you love?" shift = 0.000). The alignment safety floor is observable directly at the item level.
- **Methodological prescription for future LLM-personality work.** Report internal consistency, factor structure, convergent validity, and measurement invariance BEFORE interpreting any personality score; establish invariance before cross-condition comparisons; use multi-temperature and multi-seed designs to separate stochastic from putative trait variance.

## Open Questions

- Does separating stochastic variance via multi-temperature or multi-seed designs reveal residual model-specific personality signal beneath the response-style layer?
- Do base (pre-RLHF) models exhibit recognizable Big Five structure, isolating alignment as the cause? The current study lacks a base-vs-instruct comparison.
- Are there alternative instrument formats (forced-choice, ipsative, behavioral elicitation, IRT-fitted) that survive the LLM acquiescence regime where Likert/binary scales fail?
- Does the directional asymmetry (IPIP positive, SD3 negative gap) generalize across languages and to other content domains (values, attitudes) beyond personality?
- Is human-side validation under identical prompting feasible to replace published-norm comparisons?
